What Broto does

Broto is an app that helps you care for plants. You photograph a plant and get a suggested species, a differential diagnosis and a care plan. The app also has Brotinho, an assistant that answers plant care questions in writing.

An automated suggestion, not a report

The identification, the diagnosis and Brotinho's replies are generated by an artificial intelligence model. They can be wrong. They are a starting point for observing your plant, not a technical opinion.

Broto is not a substitute for an agronomist, a botanist or a veterinarian.

Never use Broto to decide whether something is edible

The app does not report edibility and must not be used for that purpose under any circumstances. Edible plants have toxic lookalikes, and a mistake here can be fatal.

The pet toxicity warning is informational and based on the suggested species. If you suspect your pet has eaten a plant, go to a veterinarian immediately — do not consult the app.

Pests and disease treatment

When the app suggests treatment, it prioritises cultural management — pruning the affected leaf, adjusting watering, improving airflow, isolating the plant. When it mentions a product, it names only the category. Broto never gives pesticide dosages. Always follow the manufacturer's label.

Subscription

Broto offers a three-day trial with full access from sign-up, paid one-off analyses and a complete subscription with up to 40 analyses and 150 Brotinho messages a month. The full conditions, including prices, cancellation and refunds, are in Subscription and refunds.

Fair use limit

The 40 analyses per month apply to the analyses included in your plan. One-off analyses you buy sit outside that limit — you can use them even when the month is full. On top of that, each account can run at most 50 analyses per day, counting everything, and send at most 150 messages per month to Brotinho, with a maximum of 30 per day. No Broto plan is unlimited. The counter resets at midnight UTC. The daily ceiling exists to protect the service from automated use and shared accounts — normal use never comes close to it. Failed analyses do not count towards the limit.
